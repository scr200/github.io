Senior Directed Research Study · Wilfrid Laurier University · 2026

Predictive vs. Reactive Autoscaling in Cloud Environments: A Hybrid AIOps Approach ↗

  • Python
  • Time-Series Forecasting
  • KPI Definition & Measurement
  • Experimental Design & Ablation Studies
  • LLM Integration with AI Guardrails

As a senior-level directed research study under the supervision of my professor, I investigated whether combining predictive forecasting with a Large Language Model could close the “reaction gap” that causes standard cloud autoscalers to fail during sudden traffic spikes. Using Google’s Cluster-Usage Traces, I characterized real-world workload behaviour, benchmarked forecasting models, and designed a hybrid system that reduced SLA violations by 85.2% and resource waste by 45.8% against industry baselines. I also ran an ablation study showing that without deterministic guardrails, the LLM made unsafe scaling decisions that raised resource waste to 48.7%. This project strengthened my ability to frame a research problem, design rigorous experiments, and evaluate results critically, and showed me that AI systems need strict validation to be trusted in real operations.

ST494 Statistical Learning · Team Project · Wilfrid Laurier University · 2025

Sephora Product Analysis: Brand Value Segmentation & Rating Prediction ↗

  • R
  • PCA
  • K-means Clustering
  • Random Forest
  • Logistic Regression

In a team of three for a senior statistical learning course, I analyzed a dataset of 8,494 Sephora products in R to identify which brands offer the best value and whether product ratings can be predicted from product features. Using PCA and K-means clustering, we segmented brands into luxury, good-value, affordable, and lower-value tiers, finding that price does not reliably predict customer satisfaction and that Sephora’s catalogue is weighted toward mid-priced, highly rated brands. We then built logistic regression and Random Forest models to predict ratings, reaching 67.8% accuracy but finding the models struggled to identify rare low and high ratings due to class imbalance. This project taught me that a high accuracy score can hide weak performance, and that understanding a dataset’s structure is just as important as choosing the right model.

Where to Next?

A personalized all-in-one travel hub that turns scattered bookings, recommended places, and daily plans into one clear view.

Where to Next is a personal travel app I designed and built with AI agents (Claude). It automatically pulls key booking details from Gmail, turns places saved from different sources into a route-optimized itinerary, and handles the small logistics, like reminders, getting from A to B, and what to do next.

The result: less time planning itineraries and logistics while on-the-go, targeting long-term trips where certain destinations may not always be pre-planned. Instead, it allows me to be more present and enjoy the city's best activities.

1. "Where are my bookings?" → One home for every flight and stay

  • Goal: consolidate bookings, with key details at a glance
  • Screens: 01 Today, 10–12 Bookings
  • Automatic import: flight and accommodation confirmations are pulled from Gmail automatically, with no manual entry.
  • Today: the home screen opens on a trip calendar. Flight days are marked with a plane, and every other day shows the country's flag and city. Tapping a flight opens its booking; tapping a day opens that day's plan.
  • Bookings tab: shows times, terminals, baggage allowance and confirmation codes. It can be viewed all at once or filtered by country.
  • Risk flags: the app flags issues before they become problems, such as a double-booked room, a gap night with no stay, or a tight connection on separate tickets.

2. "I have to copy everything into my calendar" → Reminders that come to me

  • Goal: reminders for important deadlines
  • Screens: 02 Reminders, 13 Add a reminder
  • Built automatically from bookings: the reminders feed covers the next 10 days, including check-ins and check-outs with times, card charge dates, free-cancellation deadlines and when to start packing.
  • Quick manual reminders: anything else takes one tap to add.
  • Phone alerts: a daily scheduled task sends a notification the day before anything important, so nothing depends on me remembering to open the app.

3. "How do I get there without overspending?" → Transport built into every day

  • Goal: optimize routes between saved places
  • Screens: 03 Itinerary, 04 Route sketch
  • Ordered by location: each day's stops are sequenced by neighbourhood and opening hours, so I'm not criss-crossing the city.
  • Getting there: between every two stops, the app shows the distance and the walking, public transport and driving times, with the most sensible option highlighted. One tap opens Google Maps with that travel mode already set.
  • Ride buttons: when a stop is too far to walk, the app shows the ride apps that operate in that country (Uber and Bolt in Spain, Grab and Be in Vietnam), so I never have to look up which app works where.
  • Route sketch: shows the day's route at a glance.

4. "Planning on the go is exhausting" → Recommendations and an itinerary I approve

  • Goals: minimize time spent searching; save from many sources
  • Screens: 06–07 For you, 14–15 Add a place, 16–18 Plan itinerary, 05 Must-sees
  • For you: a feed of researched picks for each city, matched to my travel style (slow, budget-conscious, local spots). Each card shows vibe tags, price level, distance from my stay and practical tips, like closed days and the best time to go. Filters such as Free, Art or Coffee let me decide in seconds.
  • Add a place from anywhere: type a name, paste a TikTok link, or upload a screenshot. AI reads the location tag or caption and fills in the details. I can then pick a day, let the app choose the day that best fits the route, or save it for later.
  • Plan itinerary: I set the feel of each day (rest, adventure, social or already booked) along with pace and budget. AI drafts the days around my bookings and must-sees, and I approve, swap or skip each stop. Nothing is added without my say.
  • Must-sees: starring a place marks it as a must-see. A "Don't leave without…" box tracks which ones are planned and which are done, so the essentials are covered while the rest of the day stays open.

5. Designed to stay calm

  • Goal: a simple interface that leaves room for spontaneity
  • Today first: the home screen shows only what matters today. Everything else lives in four tabs (Today, For you, Saved, Bookings).
  • Built-in free time: rest days keep deliberate gaps in the plan, and free days say so plainly: "A free day. Room to wander."

After months on the road, I encountered a familiar challenge amongst travellers: travel fatigue. Constantly juggling fragmented platforms—from booking apps to maps and social media—while adapting to new environments created an unexpected cognitive load. I wanted to minimize logistical friction so I could stay present in every destination, without sacrificing tailored, interest-driven planning.

Problems Identified:

  • Fragmented bookings: Constantly flipping between email, airline apps, and accommodation platforms to find flight times, confirmation codes, and stay addresses.
  • Manual overhead: Copying itinerary details and deadlines manually into calendars and reminder apps.
  • Logistical friction: Figuring out cost-effective, efficient transit options between unfamiliar locations.
  • Ad-hoc planning fatigue: Sifting through multiple sources on the go to find activities and consolidate them into an optimal, interest-driven itinerary.
  • Market limitations: Existing travel apps either lock core features like route optimization behind paywalls or fail to unify bookings and daily planning into a clean interface.

I scoped the product in phases. I solved the highest-friction problem first and only added features once the one before was reliable.

  1. Phase 1 (MVP):
    one home for bookings.

    What: an automated workflow pulled flight and accommodation confirmations from my Gmail into a single view, and flagged risks automatically: double-bookings, nights with no accommodation, tight connections and approaching cancellation deadlines.

    Why first: scattered bookings caused the most daily friction, and every later feature depends on knowing where I am and when.

  2. Phase 2 (V1):
    a calm daily view.

    What: a trip calendar, a day-by-day itinerary, a reminders feed, and two low-effort inputs, Add a reminder and Add a place.

    Why: it turns booking data into something I can act on each day without searching.

  3. Phase 3:
    planning help and recommendations.

    What: a recommendations feed matched to my travel style, saving places from screenshots and links, and an AI itinerary planner. These came once the daily view was stable, because good suggestions depend on knowing my bookings and saved places.

  4. Phase 4:
    transport and ride-app shortcuts.

    What: travel times between stops and shortcuts to ride apps.

    Why last: they came after the itinerary was stable, so travel times could be calculated between real stops rather than guesses.

Information hierarchy: the home screen shows only what matters today. Full booking details moved to their own tab, and secondary pages were stripped back to a single country filter, keeping the interface calm as features grew.

Human-in-the-loop AI: AI-drafted itinerary stops need my approval before they're added. I can accept a stop, ask for another, or skip it. This keeps me in control and builds trust in the recommendations.

Trade-offs within platform constraints:

Deferred to the backlog: an in-app photo carousel, live location, and saving straight from the TikTok and Instagram share menus. All three are blocked by the platform today, not by priority.

I'll measure success on my next long trip, compared against my three months of travel without the app. A 30-second daily check-in covers the self-reported measures, and the app records the behavioural ones automatically.

North Star metric:

the share of travel days where I don't need to open another app to find booking details, plan my day or work out how to get somewhere. Target: 80% or more.
